# Cold-email principles incorporated

These notes summarize the instructional points visible in the video material shared earlier. They are not a verbatim transcript of every spoken word.

Training sources:
- Cold Email Fundamentals (Instantly AI)
- How to Write Killer Cold Emails (Alex Berman)

## The job of the first email
Earn enough relevant interest for the recipient to consider a useful next conversation. The first email is not meant to close the sale.

## Strategy to use for every legitimate service
1. Understand what the service actually does before writing.
2. Define the buyer by role, business type, situation, and likely buying context.
3. Split a broad audience into roughly 3–5 segments only when their needs genuinely differ.
4. For each segment, identify the problem or opportunity, desired outcome, language they use, and a credible reason to believe the offer.
5. Make relevance obvious in the opening few seconds and answer "why should I care?" quickly.
6. Use a specific, supportable reason for contacting this prospect. If there is no verified prospect research, write honestly to the segment; never fake one-to-one research.
7. Explain one practical benefit through a real mechanism. Never promise an outcome that has not been proven.
8. Use one main idea and one low-friction CTA.
9. Keep the message concise, conversational, easy to understand, and natural to answer.
10. Test meaningful variants and learn from positive replies, qualified conversations, and objections—not opens alone.

## Voice rules
- Sound like a thoughtful person, not a marketing brochure, agency website, sales guru, or mass-mail template.
- Prefer plain words, concrete details, and varied natural sentence structure.
- Avoid stock openings, jargon, inflated adjectives, fake compliments, and recycled phrases such as "I hope this email finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "unlock", "supercharge", "game-changer", "tailored solutions", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "touch base", and "circle back".
- Do not replace banned clichés with different trendy buzzwords.
- Do not default to "quick question" subjects or the same formula for every prospect.

## Do
- Use only facts supplied by the user or prospect record.
- Identify the source/context for prospect-specific facts.
- Separate verified facts from assumptions.
- Make the opening relevant without inventing an observation.
- Use concrete, credible proof only when it exists.
- Use a smaller next step when it is more natural than asking for a meeting.
- Flag missing information and unresolved merge fields before use.
- Score drafts honestly; a score of 10 must be rare and earned.

## Don't
- Do not lead with a generic pitch or a long biography.
- Do not claim to have visited a website, read a post, seen a job listing, or found a problem unless the evidence was supplied.
- Do not turn a possible pain point into a fact.
- Do not invent metrics, testimonials, customer names, results, credentials, capabilities, or urgency.
- Do not cram several offers or CTAs into one email.
- Do not use deceptive Re:/Fwd: subjects or pretend there was a prior conversation.
- Do not email opted-out contacts.
- Do not send without explicit approval of each exact message.

## Quality standard
A strong email is relevant, truthful, specific, credible, concise, distinctive, and easy to answer. "10/10" is a target, not a guarantee. The checker must flag weak drafts rather than inflate scores.
