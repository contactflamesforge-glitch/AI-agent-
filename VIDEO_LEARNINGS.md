# Cold-email principles incorporated

These notes summarize instructional points visible in the video material shared earlier; they are not a verbatim transcript of every spoken word.

## The job of the first email
Earn enough relevant interest for the recipient to read and consider a next conversation. It is not meant to close the sale immediately.

## Do
- Make relevance obvious in the opening few seconds.
- Answer the recipient's question: "What's in it for me?"
- Divide broad audiences into roughly 3–5 meaningful segments when their needs differ.
- For each segment, understand its pain, desired outcome, language, and credible reason to trust the offer.
- Personalize with accurate, useful context.
- Keep one main idea and one low-friction call to action.
- Use concise, natural language and concrete, supportable outcomes.
- Test variants and learn from positive replies and objections.

## Don't
- Do not lead with a generic pitch or a long biography.
- Do not use fake familiarity, fake compliments, or fabricated research.
- Do not invent metrics, testimonials, customer names, results, or prospect problems.
- Do not cram several offers or CTAs into one email.
- Do not use deceptive Re:/Fwd: subjects, false urgency, or spammy hype.
- Do not email opted-out contacts.
- Do not send without explicit approval of each message.

A strong email is relevant, credible, concise, specific, and easy to answer. "10/10" is a target, not a guarantee; the checker should flag weak drafts rather than inflate scores.
